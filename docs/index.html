Curvature-based schedule ordering is preserved in homogeneous and heterogeneous tumors

bioRxiv · 10.64898/2026.11.08.765156

During treatment, dosing protocols with identical total dose may favor one schedule over another depending on how exposure is distributed over time. In homogeneous cell populations, Jensen's inequality has been shown to predict whether continuous or intermittent high-dose followed by low-dose is favored, based on the curvature of the concentration-effect function. Scheduling ordering can be performed based on curvature only: concave functions favor continuous dosing while convex functions favor intermittent dosing schedules with a high time-variance. However, tumors exhibit heterogeneous drug sensitivities, and treatment preferentially eliminates drug-sensitive cells, progressively shifting the surviving population toward greater drug resistance. Whether this treatment-induced selection can reverse curvature-based schedule ordering remains unclear. We develop a mathematical framework in which phenotypes share a common concentration-effect function but differ by fixed, nonnegative sensitivity scales. We prove that the ordering of equal-mean schedules is administrated solely by the scalar cumulative treatment effect, that is, by the curvature of the effect function alone. Consequently, baseline heterogeneity and treatment-induced selection can alter the magnitude of treatment response and the composition of surviving cells, but cannot reverse the ordering of equal-mean schedules predicted by the curvature of the single-cell concentration-effect function. This preservation holds across different temporal dosing profiles and repeated treatment cycles. Our work uncovers a general principle that curvature-based schedule ordering is preserved in heterogeneous tumors despite treatment-induced selection toward resistance.

scDeepSim: Deep Generative Simulation for Controlled Benchmarking of Single-Cell Methods

bioRxiv · 10.64898/2026.11.09.765456

Benchmarking single-cell computational methods requires both realistic data and reliable ground truth. However, annotating real datasets is costly and, even when available, may not fully represent the diverse settings where methods are used. Simulators address this gap by generating synthetic data that has fully specified ground truth and whose structure can be tailored to challenge competing methods. Existing single-cell simulators can broadly be divided into statistical and deep generative classes. Statistical simulators offer interpretable parameters for controlling simulated data, but their modeling assumptions may not accurately represent real data. Deep generative models can learn complex expression patterns, but it remains unclear how to transparently control individual biological or technical factors without introducing unintended effects. We introduce scDeepSim, a simulator that combines a structured variational autoencoder with latent diffusion to generate synthetic single-cell expression profiles. It allows users to intervene on selected biological or technical factors at user-specified strengths, producing realistic datasets with benchmark ground truth. We demonstrate this capability by benchmarking batch correction and trajectory inference methods, showing how batch-effect strength affects batch mixing and cell-type preservation, and how trajectory geometry and noise affect pseudotime recovery. scDeepSim therefore enables researchers to vary selected biological and technical factors in realistic simulated data and assess how these changes affect the performance of single-cell analysis methods, helping understand when such methods may be appropriately applied. Code for the scDeepSim simulator and experiments is available at https://github.com/LangtianM/scDeepSim.
