When should adaptation arise from a polygenic response versus few large-effect changes?

PLOS Biology · 10.1371/journal.pbio.3004119

When will adaptation involve a few genetic changes of large effect versus a polygenic response? There are compelling reasons to expect polygenic adaptation to be common, yet direct evidence is lacking. Conversely, there are hundreds of examples of large-effect adaptations across species, but it is unclear whether they occur frequently within any given species. These different lines of evidence are hard to synthesize, in part due to the lack of theory that relates them. To address this gap, we frame the question in terms of the properties of an adaptive trait. We ask how the genetic basis of adaptation depends on key aspects of genetic variation in the trait and on the degree to which its selection pressures have changed. To this end, we consider a quantitative trait subject to stabilizing selection and model its response when a population at mutation–selection–drift balance experiences a sudden shift in the trait optimum. Using this model, we delimit how the contributions of large-effect and polygenic changes to adaptation depend on the genetics and ecology of adaptive traits, as well as on other salient factors. Thus, we formulate testable predictions for when different modes of adaptation are expected.

Caveolin-1-dependent signaling and survival in distinct Ewing sarcoma cell states.

bioRxiv · 10.1101/2024.09.23.614468

Plasticity is a hallmark function of cancer cells, yet the mechanisms that enable dynamic switching between survival states remain incompletely understood. Here, we identify Caveolin-1, a membrane-domain scaffolding protein, as a regulator of survival signaling in a Ewing sarcoma (EwS) cell line. Single-cell analyses reveal a distinct subpopulation of EwS cells marked by high CD99 and elevated Caveolin-1 expression. These CD99High cells exhibit unique morphology, transcriptional programs, and markedly enhanced survival both under chemotherapeutic challenge and in vivo. Importantly, CD99High and CD99Low states are reversible, providing EwS cells with a flexible route to survival-oriented plasticity. Mechanistically, we show that Caveolin-1 in CD99High cells orchestrates PI3K/AKT survival signaling by modulating recruitment of PI3K activity to the plasma membrane. We propose that the CD99High state establishes a Caveolin-1-driven signaling architecture that supports survival through mechanisms distinct from those used by CD99Low cells. These findings uncover a dynamic state transition in EwS cells and position Caveolin-1 as a regulator of cell-state-dependent survival signaling.
