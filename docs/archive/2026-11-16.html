Dynamic multi-omics analysis of Adenovirus-induced rAAV-producing stable cell lines reveals the interplay between adenoviral infection and host cell metabolism

bioRxiv · 10.64898/2026.11.12.766245

Recombinant adeno-associated viruses (rAAV) are invaluable delivery vehicles for gene therapy. However, to make rAAV-based therapies more accessible, there is a need to increase rAAV titers. Here we analyze the phenotypic variation among 30 Adenovirus-induced stably-transfected human producer cell clones to identify metabolic signatures associated with high vs low rAAV production. Exometabolomic characterization revealed decreased amino acid consumption in higher producers. Analysis of RNA-Seq data in the context of a genome-scale metabolic model identified upregulated carnosine biosynthesis-associated pathways during the first 6 hours post infection with adenovirus, relative to uninfected growth. Meanwhile, late infection timepoints exhibited increased amino acid release and de novo biosynthesis of serine, glycine, and arginine. Moreover, high producers decreased expression of genes linked with cholesterol biosynthesis and increased HMG-CoA degradation via HMGCL. Furthermore, sphingosine and ceramide biosynthesis genes were altered in high-producers in directions previously reported to resist cell death and suppress immune signaling. Overall, these findings elucidate the metabolic modulation upon adenoviral infection in a helper virus-induced AAV production process using stably transfected human cell lines.

Identification of JMJD1C as a key mediator of fibroblast remodelling in human AF by single-nucleus multi-omics

bioRxiv · 10.64898/2026.11.12.765924

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most prevalent arrhythmia, accounting for up to 15% of arrhythmias in the elderly. Atrial enlargement associated with fibrotic and hypertrophic remodelling, termed atrial myopathy (AM), is considered a precursor to AF development. The mechanisms underlying the development of AM and its progression to AF remain incompletely resolved. To identify the unique and combined contributions of the major cellular mediators of AM and AF, we combine single-nucleus transcriptomics of human left and right atrial appendages (LAA and RAA) with paired RNA and chromatin-accessibility profiling of 20 LAA samples. We validate these findings through histological and functional studies in primary human cardiac fibroblasts (HCFs). Patients were stratified according to the presence of AF and AM (identified by left atrial volume dimensions; LAVI, Hi and Lo), generating Hi-AF, Hi-SR, Lo-AF and Lo-SR groups that separate AF-associated from structural-remodelling-associated alterations. Across 75,654 qualified nuclei, AF was associated with chamber-dependent shifts in cellular composition and gene expression, with extracellular matrix (ECM) and TGF-beta programmes prominent in LAA. Multiome analysis identified a stressed atrial cardiomyocyte (aCM4) state expressing natriuretic peptide A (NPPA) and natriuretic peptide B (NPPB), with altered cis-regulatory connectivity, and an inflammatory fibroblast (PTGDS+ FB) state characterised by IL-6 expression and matrix- and TGF-beta-associated programmes. Enhancer-driven network analysis identified GLIS3, TWIST2 and TBX20 as regulators of FB state transitions. The histone demethylase JMJD1C was enriched in PTGDS+ FB and induced by TGF-beta; its depletion attenuated alpha-smooth-muscle-actin (alpha-SMA) accumulation and reprogrammed TGF-beta-responsive transcription. Ligand-receptor analysis further predicted macrophage-to-FB TGF-beta signalling, showing the contribution of macrophages to the atrial inflammatory environment. These data define discrete aspects of rhythm- and structure-associated remodelling of human atrial tissue and identify a candidate macrophage-TGF-beta-JMJD1C-FB interaction for potential therapeutic development.
